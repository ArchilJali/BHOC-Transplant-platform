Pig-to-human kidney transplantation: two studies, different questions.

From an early 54-hour observation to a later 61-day study. Keep the research model, observation period and publication history visible.

BHOC Transplant editorial synthesis · Published and source-checked 27 September 2026

Evidence boundary: these are external xenotransplantation studies. Neither tested BHOC or an oxygen-carrier perfusate. Their inclusion identifies questions for transplantation research, not BHOC efficacy or safety.

NEJM 2022Nature 2026CorrectionsBHOC perspectiveSources
TR-REL-2022-006 · Kidney · Decedent research

The early study: short-term function

Montgomery RA, Stern JM, Lonze BE, et al. Results of Two Cases of Pig-to-Human Kidney Xenotransplantation. NEJM, 19 May 2022;386:1889–1898. Full author list and abstract.

Two recipients with established brain death received genetically modified pig kidney xenografts and were observed for 54 hours. The authors reported urine production and no biopsy evidence of hyperacute or antibody-mediated rejection during that interval. [1]

Interpretation: this supports short-term feasibility in the reported model. It does not establish durable function, long-term safety or outcomes in living patients. The observation window is part of the result, not a detail to omit.

TR-REL-2026-007 · Kidney · Decedent research

The later study: function and immune response

Montgomery RA, Stern JM, Fathi F, Suek N, et al. Physiology and immunology of a pig-to-human decedent kidney xenotransplant. Nature 650:218–229. First online 13 November 2025; issue date 5 February 2026. Full author list and abstract.

One recipient with established brain death was studied for a planned 61 days after a gene-edited pig kidney transplant. Dialysis independence was achieved. Antibody-mediated rejection developed on day 33; the authors reported reversal after treatment. [2]

Interpretation: 61 days of observation is not 61 rejection-free days. This single decedent study cannot establish long-term outcomes in living recipients. The two reports are not a controlled comparison, and their durations should not be presented as a treatment-effect estimate.

Read the correction trail

The Nature record links a Publisher Correction dated 12 February 2026 and an Author Correction dated 4 June 2026. These are amendments to the same study, not two additional studies. [3] [4]

The publisher correction changes figure-axis units and an immunoglobulin label. Readers using figure-level findings should consult both notices and the current publisher version rather than an earlier downloaded manuscript. This brief does not reproduce or reanalyse those figures. [3]

Where this fits in the BHOC research journey

Our interpretation, not the studies' conclusion: organ-source research and the preservation of organ function deserve separate, connected questions. For BHOC Transplant, the relevant next question is whether a characterized oxygen carrier could support measured graft function under defined conditions. Neither report answers that question.

Before proposing a role in a xenograft pathway, we would need to distinguish donor-animal genetic modification, immune management and ex-vivo oxygen support. These are not interchangeable interventions. A BHOC evaluation would need its own comparison, organ-specific endpoints and safety assessment.

For the organ preparation centre, our proposed focus is measured function rather than time alone. For implantation and reperfusion, ex-vivo results should not be substituted for evidence on treatment of a recipient. The Science page sets out the separate oxygen-affinity and metabolic questions.
